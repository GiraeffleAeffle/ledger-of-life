# Roadmap: where we are and what is next

Position as of 29 September 2026. This is the one page that says where the project stands; [implementation status](IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md) keeps the detailed evidence and [the plan](LEDGER_OF_LIFE_PLAN.md) keeps the product story. Wording follows the ontology's [reality levels](ontology.yaml): nothing in the app is at `live_mainnet`.

## Destination and deadline

**Find a home, keep your assets, help build your city** ([plan](LEDGER_OF_LIFE_PLAN.md)). The demo story is: home, share-backed cash, a local stake, see the place, use the local GPU.

The Crypto World's Fair (Colosseum) closes **12 October 2026, 23:59 Pacific (13 October, 08:59 Berlin)**: 13 days from today. Winners are announced by 5 December. Nothing has been submitted ([README](../README.md)). The repository has no licence file, and the rules score open-source; prior work (the Gnosis "Smart Rental Deposit" prototype) must be disclosed at submission.

## The frontier

Order matters where a blocker is named. Each item is a decision or a vertical slice that ends in something demonstrable.

1. **Decide the demo track and freeze scope** (decision D1 below). Blocks 6, 8 and 9.
2. **Choose a licence, write the prior-work disclosure, submit before the deadline** (D2). No blocker. Deadline-critical.
3. **Stable HTTPS origin, hosted PostgreSQL, secrets and a scheduler** (D3). Blocks 4, 6 and 10. Live since 30 September at `https://ledger.stadtstack.eu`: Privy allows the origin, the image is public and pinned by digest, and the owner ran the release wrapper (Helm revision 1). A passkey sign-up works on the hosted origin and the reconcile job runs every minute. The owner accepted SQLite on a volume without backup for the contest window. **Revision 2, live the same day, real flows only (the owner's decision):** the Solana rental deposit with a fee-only sponsor key used by this host alone (`/api/status` reports the Solana RPC, deployment and sponsor as configured); self-service test money (the person's own wallet mints tUSDG; public faucets give test ETH, the official test TSLA and devnet USDC); Local stakes (the public manifest is now in the image); no false atlas outage; anti-framing headers; and a probe that proved the network policies are enforced. The operator test tools stay off in public. **Loans and landlord earnings, rebuilt the same day:** one shared market on Robinhood Chain testnet takes the official test TSLA at a price copied from Chainlink RHTSLA/USD on mainnet (feed `0x5196C8713A529bd676B875fB9Cea3F8a47ba48Be`, pool `0xA9c03045FD317230ab811419D13Fd4D0652DE069`, both verified on the testnet explorer; no owner, 10,000 test dollars seeded to a burn address, first price $350.463). Lenders earn what borrowers pay; the per-wallet market with a fake stock and its levers is gone everywhere. It reaches the public site with the next image; the paid AI desk returns with the host connector (branch `codex/host-connector`).
4. **Stable-origin recovery and a cancelled-signature rehearsal.** Blocked by 3.
5. **Real-Privy Home journey on the pinned program, including a contested claim.** Needs the pinned manifest, a funded sponsor and tenant test USDC. Blocks 7 and 9.
6. **The worker reconciles every lane and runs payouts unattended.** Today it scans one configured lane, and payouts only run while a party's browser is open. Blocked by 3.
7. **An actual surplus and an earnings release with Privy**, either the labelled simulated credit or a real reserve. Blocked by 5.
8. **The exit leg (sell, personal-cash withdrawal) or an explicit "unproved" label.** Real provider access is gated outside our control. Blocked by 1.
9. **Acceptance replay on the chosen track**: interruption recovery, accessible confirmations, no unlabelled simulated result. Blocked by 1, 5, 6 and 8.
10. **Home Assistant on a hosted build.** Done for safety (29 September): a production build never fetches a Home Assistant address unless the operator sets `ALLOW_HOME_ASSISTANT_PULL=1` (local setup sets it), so the [Codex review](CODEX_REVIEW_2026-09-26.md)'s last open finding is closed and every one of its twelve findings is fixed in code. A hosted demo therefore shows no solar. To show it there, the home pushes a few numbers to the app (design in D3); that is not built.
11. **The ten UX and flow problems** in the [29 September audit](research/UX_FLOW_AUDIT_2026-09-29.md). Fixed: 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9. Mostly: 4 and 10. Partly: 2 and 8. Built on 30 September: cancelling a tenancy (landlord or tenant) before the deposit is locked; refused while escrow initialization is confirming, while funding is signed, broadcast or unknown, and once the deposit is funded. What is left needs a decision or a program change: a one-person hosted journey (D3, D6), replacing a party after a tenant is chosen, and a dispute timeout. None of the fixes has been run with a funded wallet or a second real browser.
12. **Getting onboarded into a city** (D7). A first slice is built: `/welcome/strausberg`, a public page with no account. It needs a decision on what comes next: a German version, a handout at the registration desk, more cities, and an account-free Explore of Places and Today.

Done in this pass: one home per feature ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-one-home-per-feature.md)), Money in four sections, verified adapter explanations, the first-run guide, the subtotal shown as free / locked / pledged / owed (deposit stays inside, labelled locked), the recovery proof moved to the first wallet action ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-ask-for-recovery-at-the-first-wallet-action.md)), a Home that says a tenancy needs three accounts and walks through it, and a TSLA buy that takes an amount and a review. `/design` (development only) renders real components with invented numbers so a layout can be judged without an account.
Also fixed on 30 September: Today's first-run guide told a person who had already chosen a city to choose it again. Two causes. (1) Any failed read was drawn the same way as "not done"; a step whose read failed now says "We could not check this just now" with a **Try again** button, is never offered as the next step, and never hides progress already made (tests in `setup-steps.test.ts`). (2) The real trigger: the city reads were shared across the whole page, and a read started just before the account's session settled (right after sign-up, when the wallets arrive) was handed to the new session, whose request helper then rejects it as "Account changed". Reads are now shared only within one session (`shared-reads.ts`, with tests); a fresh sign-up in a browser shows "Choose your city" as the next step.

Later, none needed for the deadline: welcome vouchers, verified venue coordinates, Strausberg's detailed budget, meter and invoice inputs, a licensed partner for real local investments, a deterministic plan-versus-actual guard for city interpretations.

## Decisions only the owner can make

- **D1 · Demo track.** Solana has the fuller Home journey proof; Robinhood has the connected borrow → local stakes → paid AI proof with Privy. The [Colosseum rules](https://colosseum.com/legal/Crypto%20World%27s%20Fair%20Hackathon%20Rules.pdf) allow one submission per team (§7) and list a Solana track ($100,000 across 10 products) and a Robinhood Chain track ($25,000 across 5) (§14). They say nothing about using several chains or entering two tracks; the public FAQ says every submission is eligible for the general prizes whatever the infrastructure, and track prizes come on top. Whether one project can win both tracks is unknown: ask Colosseum. Recommended: one product, each chain doing what its evidence shows (see the next section). Also unclear: §12(a)(i) says all Content must be in English, and the app cites German city sources.
- **D2 · Licence.** The contracts already carry MIT in one file. Pick one for the repository (MIT or Apache-2.0 are the usual choices) so the open-source criterion has something to score.
- **D3 · Where it runs. Direction chosen 29 September: the owner's Talos cluster on Hetzner, a domain and HTTPS ingress. Hostname chosen 30 September: `ledger.stadtstack.eu`.** A stable origin is needed for passkeys, recovery and the scheduler. Privy's allowed-origins list is a security recommendation for production (its default is permissive) and can be edited at any time. Its docs treat production as its own app: a new app starts without the development app's accounts, while an app client under the same app would keep them. A passkey made on `localhost` cannot be used on another domain (WebAuthn scopes it to the domain). Which domain Privy registers, the exact host or its parent, is decided by Privy's server (the SDK sends `window.origin`) and is not documented in what was read. Agreed so far:
  - **Two modes from one codebase.** A hosted demo (test networks, as little stored as possible) for judges, and local mode (`npm run setup`, everything on the owner's LAN) as the privacy-first way to run it. The rental journey between three people needs a shared server, so it exists only in the hosted mode.
  - **The GPU is never public, and one owner-run GPU is not the design. Built and revised after code/security review on 30 September, released the same day and proven live that evening (own compute and a paid city answer); not independently audited.** Local mode keeps `LOCAL_AI_OLLAMA_URL` and its direct inference lease. Hosted mode leaves that URL empty and uses paired outbound hosts, without app-initiated LAN connections or residents joining the operator's VPN.
    - **Outbound host connector.** A zero-runtime-dependency Node relay (`host-connector/`, [ADR 0013](adr/0013-outbound-host-connector.md)) creates an owner-only Ed25519 key, signs HTTPS polls/results/heartbeats, and forwards bounded text-only chat to configured Ollama. It runs no tools, shell or browsing. The app never dials the host; existing public-HTTPS-only cluster egress remains unchanged. The worktree smoke returned a synthetic stub answer through the actual app routes and connector, then cleared visitor text; the owner's desktop, Ollama and Home Assistant were not contacted.
    - **Onboarding now built.** An account with one verified EVM wallet in `LOCAL_AI_HOST_OWNER_WALLETS` creates a private twelve-character invitation in Money → Devices & income → Pair hosts & income and chooses its verified payout. The code is shown once, stored hashed, expires in ten minutes and is single-use. The connector consumes it with a locally generated public key and registers directly to that account. Creation uses the verified session, not fresh per-action passkey reauthentication. Heartbeats show online, asleep or offline; public status contains caller-relative ownership rather than account DIDs. Direct local mode hides connector controls. The owner's wake default is tokenless UDP Wake-on-LAN (`34:5A:60:69:E2:73`, LAN broadcast `192.168.178.255`); optional Home Assistant needs HTTPS or an explicit trusted-LAN plaintext opt-in and a dedicated non-admin local token. Still not built: fixed-prompt speed qualification, trial/listed promotion, speed ranking and cross-host spot-checking.
    - **Payment.** Own paired-host compute is free: no authorization, payout or receipt, even with a single EVM wallet and no token balances/facilitator. Third-party answers use x402 v2 `upto`/Permit2 at 100 atomic tUSDG (0.0001 test USD) per output token on Robinhood testnet. The authorization maximum is `maxOutputTokens × 100` atomic tUSDG; actual reported output tokens determine settlement to the selected host only after a saved complete answer with `done: true` and `done_reason: "stop"`. Token-limit, incomplete, interrupted or failed inference costs nothing. The 0.10-tUSDG approval and dedicated fee key stay unchanged; signed `to`, `facilitator` and `validAfter` restrict settlement through `0x4020A4f3b7b90ccA423B9fabCc0CE57C6C240002` to the named facilitator, payee, maximum and validity window. No platform fee; real-money third-party service still needs stablecoins and legal review.
    - **Implemented shape.** Durable key/owner/payout/model/heartbeat/state registry; authenticated same-origin owner invitations/revoke; bounded invitation-consuming pairing; signed poll/result/heartbeat. Invalid public requests cannot spend shared invitation or host capacity; coarse per-source limits follow body/key validation. Signature headers are checked before reading, with three-second body deadlines. Own hosts are preferred; city routing requires an explicitly public question. Host slots are reserved before state/quota changes; busy or unavailable requests remain ready to resume. Abandoned polls release their slot without claiming jobs. The one-replica queue discards copies at completion, timeout, revocation, clear or restart; visitor clearing interrupts requests, purges both text fields and fences late persistence. Full lint and 351 root tests (including connector CI tests), plus 15 connector-package tests, passed. Updated end-to-end smoke exercised invitation, asleep host, loopback-only tokenless wake, complete synthetic answer, body precheck/deadline, text clear and code-free connector restart (139 ms answer). See [deployment requirements](DEPLOYMENT.md#the-gpu).
    - **Sleeping-host and cold-model contract.** Jobs have a 240 s total lifetime, including wake, readiness and explicit model preload; pickup remains 30 s and generation is at most 90 s within the remaining lifetime. The connector must respect the existing `expiresAt` without changing `{id, model, messages, options, expiresAt}`. Saved resumable running requests return promptly for client polling; ingress stays at least 120 s, with no new signature or authorization. Real sleeping-GPU validation and release of this change are not claimed here.
    - **Limits, stated up front.** The host reads questions in clear and may keep copies; a host cannot be made to forget text already fetched. LAN HTTP Ollama also permits anyone on that transport to read questions or forge complete answers. Payment gating and signatures cannot prove a model or authenticate that plaintext hop. Models, wake ability and usage are host claims. Contest invitations are not residence proof (D7). Hosted third-party paid settlement was proven on 30 September under the former flat-price path (a second account paid 0.01 tUSDG for an answer through the connector, [deployment](DEPLOYMENT.md)); the first per-token `upto` settlement followed on 1 October (revision 17, 104 tokens, 0.0104 tUSDG, [evidence](evidence/HOSTED_PER_TOKEN_AI_ANSWER_ROBINHOOD_TESTNET_2026-10-01.json)); the host woke from sleep and answered after about 68 s, but a wake inside the connector's 30-second pickup is not shown. One replica only; multi-replica shared queues/coordinated leases remain unbuilt.
  - **Public city AI is for anyone, not a residence-gated allowance.** A public release may set `LOCAL_AI_LIBRARY_ENABLED=1`; it enables both connector and direct free library mode. Free access permits 30 shared attempts per UTC day and three per visitor. Connector free requests require explicit public-question consent and use only hosts whose owner enabled `freePublicAnswers` (default `false`), with no payment or payout. Paid third-party answers cost 100 atomic tUSDG per output token only on a complete stop, bounded by the signed `maxOutputTokens × 100` maximum; own compute is unchanged. Residence is not checked. This policy is not a claim that the new behavior has been released.
  - **Questions and answers are not kept. Built 29 September.** The database keeps a question and its answer only while an answer is running, settling or has a payment in flight, then removes both after `LOCAL_AI_TEXT_GRACE_SECONDS` (600 by default). The reconcile job's `local-ai` scope enforces it; a read also removes overdue text; "Finish & clear this desk" removes a visitor's text at once. Usage counts, timings and receipts stay. On SQLite the text is also gone from the database file and its log: a first end-to-end run found 20 copies still in the log after the API said "removed", so the store now sets `secure_delete` and the job truncates the log (a byte-level test guards it). Postgres would keep dead rows until autovacuum. The model host still sees each question in clear while it runs.
  - **Home Assistant is never exposed. Pull is off on hosted builds (built 29 September); push is designed, not built.** The old design (the server holds a full-power token, reads every entity of the home and fetches a user-supplied address) cannot go on a shared cluster, and its address check is bypassable. Push design: the person creates a write-only token for their own ledger (shown once, stored hashed, revocable); a Home Assistant automation POSTs at most six numbers on a schedule (solar kWh today, solar W now, consumption kWh today, optional savings today, month and year); the app validates ranges and timestamps, limits size and rate, and keeps the latest reading plus daily totals for as long as a service-charge statement needs them, with a "forget my readings" control. Every push-fed figure says "reported by your home, not checked here". A leaked token can only write false numbers into that one person's ledger; it can read nothing and reaches nothing at home. The server never fetches an address a person supplied. Local runs keep the pull, since that server is on the person's own network.
  - **Packaging. Built 29 September.** `Dockerfile` (multi-stage, non-root, `/data` volume, health check on the store), a deny-by-default `.dockerignore`, `NEXT_OUTPUT=standalone` for the image only (local `npm start` is unchanged), and a lockfile that `npm ci` accepts on Linux (it was missing two optional packages, which also broke CI's install). Keys and `contracts/evm/out` are supplied at run time, never baked in; since 30 September the image contains two public deployment manifests on Robinhood Chain testnet, Local stakes and the shared loan market, because the app reads them at fixed paths. The production build no longer uses `--webpack` (36.8 s → 15.3 s). Built for `linux/amd64` (the cluster's architecture) in 144 s under emulation and run read-only with all capabilities dropped: healthy in 8 s.

  - **Places pilot scope, 1 October:** the community section links this English-only public guide. Council lists use published OParl records for the seven covered cities, with scheduled/publication dates, official links and body identifiers when names are absent; Strausberg remains links-only. Its citywide 2025/26 ordinance plan reconciles administrative + investment + financing cash outlays to €83,708,074 / €78,540,189, beside “actual: not published” in the reviewed snapshot. Ordinance, inspection and AIG sources remain linked; this is not a detailed spending breakdown or a current-amendment tracker. News distinguishes koeln.de events from city press and shows the publication snapshot date. Wider geographic levels remain portal links. Catalogue statuses await hosted evidence.
  - **How the content was checked.** Every quoted evidence snippet was compared with its live page. Of 30 steps and contacts, 27 matched exactly and 3 differed only in formatting (a stray markdown mark, raw HTML, a space before a full stop). All 38 remaining groups match their cited pages. The first pass flagged 8 groups; one was dropped (its site returns 404), one was re-sourced, three baths summaries were rewritten to what their pages say, one tenants' association was re-linked over plain http because its https certificate fails, and two turned out to be limits of the checker. Clubs whose own sites are inactive or unverifiable were removed (the 1. SchwimmClub's site says it is no longer active), so 27 of the 61 leads in the residency project's directory survive. A permanent test guards the promises: every claim cites a page and a date, official means a government host, no private phone number or email, no step is hidden.
  - **Public information stays public.** News, what the city is deciding, clubs and events need no account and no proof, because gating public data behind an identity adds friction and a profile of what each person reads. Proof of residence gates only *rights*: processes a municipality limits to residents and resident-first local offers; public city AI is available to anyone without a residence check. Levels: (0) anyone reads, with no account; (1) an account with a self-declared city and interests kept on the device, which tune what a person sees and unlock nothing; (2) a verified resident, meaning `resident_city` or `resident_postal_code` only, disclosed selectively from the EU wallet, of which the app keeps only "resident of <municipality>, until <date>"; (3) a licensed partner's own identity check for regulated investing, which this app does not perform.
  - **Residence proof, reality today.** The German wallet app launches in early 2027 (sandbox only until then), residence attributes are optional in the PID, relying parties must be state-registered, and the prototype uses the EU test verifier, so residence proof is a labelled test badge for the contest. The residency project already decided to build local eligibility first ("do not wait for EUDI"), and its ZK layer keeps later participation unlinkable to identity: reuse it.
  - **Not done, and needing a decision:** a German version (the app and the Colosseum rules are English, real newcomers mostly read German; the owner deferred German and other languages on 30 September); the registration-desk handout or QR card (a card only helps once someone hands it out, and handed out in Strausberg it needs German, so it follows the German version); more cities; an account-free Explore of Places and Today; live facility status such as "the pool reopened" (the baths publish it on their own site, and the published feed does not collect it); and a check with the city that the guide is right.
